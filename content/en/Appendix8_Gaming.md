# Appendix 8: Gaming

> After all that talk, we finally get to the most exciting part – gaming. This appendix covers how to use major gaming platforms, account security, and a few useful features.

## 8.1 Steam

Steam is the world’s largest digital game distribution platform. Most PC games can be bought here.

### 8.1.1 Finding the Official Website

The official [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/) website is `store.steampowered.com`. When you search for “Steam” in China, you might see a site called “Steam China” (`steamchina.com`), which is the domestic version operated by Tencent. Its game library and account system are separate from the global Steam – it is not recommended. Always look for the `steampowered.com` domain.

If you cannot open this website, you can use the Watt Toolkit (introduced below) to accelerate access.

### 8.1.2 Creating an Account

When registering, it is advisable to use a more secure email address, such as Outlook, Gmail, or ProtonMail. QQ email has a relatively higher risk of being stolen, and losing your Steam account can be a big loss.

You can also download the Steam mobile app for 2FA authentication.

During registration you may encounter Google’s reCAPTCHA. If it doesn’t load, the Watt Toolkit mentioned below can also help.

### 8.1.3 [Watt Toolkit](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9mtcfhs560ng) (formerly Steam++)

This is a free, open‑source, multi‑function Steam toolbox. You can download it from GitHub or its official website. Main features:

- Network acceleration: speeds up access to Steam Community, GitHub, Google CAPTCHA, Ubisoft Connect, etc.
- Account switching: quickly switch between multiple logged‑in Steam accounts.
- Inventory management: directly manage your game inventory, edit game information (name, cover, background, etc.).
- Idle card farming: automatically farm Steam trading cards.

It is completely free and ad‑free, much more practical than many paid boosters.

### 8.1.4 Steam Account Contents

- **Steam Points**: You earn points when you buy games (roughly 100+ points for every 10 RMB spent). Points can be used to decorate your profile (avatar frames, backgrounds, animated stickers), buy chat emoticons, post “funny” awards on reviews, etc.
- **Steam Level**: Raised by crafting badges or spending money. Higher levels increase your friend list limit and give more profile showcases. Not very meaningful for the average player.

### 8.1.5 On Changing Regions

Changing your Steam account to another region (e.g., Argentina, Turkey, low‑price regions) is not recommended. Steam officially cracks down on cross‑region purchases. At best, you will be forced back to your home region; at worst, your account may be banned. The China region is already one of the low‑price regions globally, and most games are reasonably priced – no need to go through the hassle.

### 8.1.6 Family Sharing

Steam Family Sharing allows you to share your game library with up to 5 family members. After enabling it, your friends can play games from your library and vice versa, provided that you are not playing the same game at the same time (playing different games at the same time is fine).

How to enable: Steam Settings → Family → check “Authorise Library Sharing on this computer”, then check the accounts you want to share with.

Note: Some games (especially those that require an additional third‑party account) do not support sharing. Saves are separate and do not affect each other’s progress.

### 8.1.7 Remote Play Together

For local multiplayer games like *It Takes Two* or *Overcooked*, Remote Play Together lets you invite a friend to play online – your friend does not need to own the game.

How to use:
1. The person who owns the game launches it.
2. In the Steam friends list, right‑click on your friend → select “Remote Play Together”.
3. After your friend accepts, the screen is streamed to them, and you play together in split‑screen.

Your friend only needs a stable internet connection and a Steam account – no purchase necessary.

## 8.2 Epic Games Store

[Epic](https://store.epicgames.com/) is Steam’s main competitor, operated by the makers of *Fortnite* and Unreal Engine. Its biggest draw is the weekly free games.

Official website: `epicgames.com`. You can log in with a Steam account or register separately.

**Free games**: Every Thursday, one or more free games are updated. Once claimed, they stay in your library permanently. Past free games have included *GTA5*, *Civilization VI*, *Batman*, and other big titles. It’s a good idea to install the Epic client and log in weekly to claim them – they add up over time.

# Extended Reading

> The following sections cover more advanced tips for Steam and Epic.

## Extended 1: Steam Refund Policy

If you have played a game for less than 2 hours and bought it within the last 14 days, you can request a no‑questions‑asked refund. The refund is returned to your original payment method (or to your Steam Wallet). This is useful for games you try and do not like.

Refund path: Steam Support → select the game → “I would like a refund”.

## Extended 2: Steam Wishlist and Discount Notifications

When you add a game to your wishlist, Steam will send you an email when it goes on sale. During major sales (Summer Sale, Winter Sale, Lunar New Year Sale), many games are 50–80% off. It is best to wait for these sales before buying.

## Extended 3: Steam Cloud Saves

Steam enables cloud saves by default. Your game saves are automatically synced to the server. When you log in on another computer, your saves are downloaded automatically. Some older games may not support this. You can view and manage cloud saves in the game’s properties.

## Extended 4: Epic and Steam Interoperability

Some games support cross‑platform play (e.g., *Rocket League*, *Fall Guys*). However, Epic and Steam are two separate platforms; their game libraries are not shared. Buying the same game on both platforms does not let you share progress, unless the game itself supports cross‑platform saves.

## Extended 5: Epic Coupons and Cashback

Epic sometimes distributes $10 coupons (for games priced at $14.99 or more), which can let you get games at very low prices when combined with discounts. Additionally, Epic has an “Epic Rewards” cashback program – 5% of each purchase is returned as Epic Rewards (usable for future purchases).

## Extended 6: Game Activation Keys

Steam activation keys purchased from third‑party sites (e.g., Satoru, Humble Bundle) can be redeemed in the Steam client via “Add a Game” → “Activate a Product on Steam”. Be careful about the source – do not buy from unknown sellers or black‑market key resellers (keys may be revoked or your account banned).

## Extended 7: Steam Big Picture Mode

Steam has a built‑in Big Picture Mode (the controller icon), with an interface optimised for controllers and TVs. It is great for playing in the living room with a controller. It can also be used to stream games to a tablet via the Steam Link app.
