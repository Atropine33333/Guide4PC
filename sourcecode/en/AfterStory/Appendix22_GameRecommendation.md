# Appendix 22: Game Recommendations

> This appendix lists games I genuinely like, sorted by category. These are purely personal tastes – take them as suggestions, not gospel.  
> I do not play competitive games, and my setup cannot handle the latest high‑spec blockbusters, so this list only covers games I can actually run, have played, and truly enjoyed. If your tastes align with mine, this may be useful. If not, treat it as a casual glimpse.

## Visual Novels (VN)

**Ever17 -the out of infinity-**

A classic sci‑fi mystery VN developed by KID and written by Kotaro Uchikoshi. The player is trapped in the underwater theme park "LeMU" and must escape before the 119‑hour countdown reaches zero. The story alternates between two protagonists (a teenage boy and a young man) across four character routes. The payoff comes at the end of the final route – a few minutes of revelations that recontextualise everything you thought you knew, leaving your scalp tingling.

**9-nine- (Complete Series)**

An battle romance AVG from PALETTE, split into four episodes plus a finale. Set in the city of Batsubutsugawa after an earthquake gave rise to "artifacts" that grant supernatural abilities. Protagonist Shinji watches as the city's ordinary life unravels. The first two episodes are standard moe‑style paranormal battles. The third episode takes off, and by the fourth (Snow of the White Snow), the writer pulls the player themselves into the story in a way that is quietly devastating. The fourth wall of the visual novel genre is shattered, and you never even noticed it being built.

**ATRI -My Dear Moments-**

A short, all‑ages visual novel published by Aniplex.EXE. In a post‑apocalyptic world of rising seas, a boy named Natsuki salvages a robot girl, Atri, from the ocean floor. The high‑spirited "high‑performance robo‑daughter" greets him with "Master!" and fills their daily seaside life with warmth and laughter. But three diaries reveal the truth – all that cheerful everyday life is a setup for a farewell. In just eight hours, it tells a complete arc of meeting, bonding, parting, and a reunion spanning decades.

**Summer Pockets REFLECTION BLUE**

Key's long‑form, all‑ages crying game. Set on the island of Torishiro in the Seto Inland Sea. Protagonist Hairi goes to pack up his late grandmother's belongings and ends up spending his summer with the island's girls. "Summer", "nostalgia", and "memory" are the central themes. REFLECTION BLUE adds four new routes and a massive amount of new CG – it is the definitive edition and a culmination of Key's later work.

**eden\***

An all‑ages visual novel from minori. No choices – a single, straight path. With Earth about to be destroyed, a super‑weapon "Felix" girl named Sion spends her final days delivering humanity's last evacuation ship. When the order comes to evacuate, the protagonist, Ryou, chooses to stay behind on the dying planet and accompany her to the very end. Presented in full cinematic widescreen, with music by Tenmon. It is about "goodbye", executed with absolute purity and romance.

## Triple‑A Games

**GTA5**

Need I say more? Los Santos. The single‑player campaign's three protagonists (Michael, Trevor, Franklin) deliver a masterclass in love‑hate relationships. The online mode is a criminal empire that can swallow thousands of hours.

**GTA6**

Confirmed by Take‑Two – scheduled for November 19, 2026, on PS5 and Xbox Series.

**Red Dead Redemption 2**

One of the highest achievements in open‑world storytelling. 1899, the dying days of the American frontier. Arthur Morgan's struggle between loyalty to his gang and his own redemption. Slow‑paced, dense with detail, and capable of absorbing hundreds of hours. Rockstar co‑founder Dan Houser called it "the best work he's ever been part of".

**Cyberpunk 2077**

A disastrous launch, reborn after two years. Night City's density and immersion are unique in open‑world games. Version 2.0 and the *Phantom Liberty* DLC rebuilt the game from the ground up – it is now what was promised. If you are new, play the base game first, then watch the spin‑off anime *Edgerunners*. It hits hard.

## Indie Games

**Disco Elysium**

A detective RPG with no traditional combat – everything is driven by dialogue and skill checks. You are an amnesiac detective tasked with investigating a hanged corpse outside the Whirling‑in‑Rags. Your stat points go into things like "Inland Empire", "Hand/Eye Coordination", and "Encyclopedia" – each skill is a voice in your head, and they argue with each other. The script won a Hugo Award, the only game ever to do so. Save often – you can die in very strange ways.

**Rain World**

You are a slugcat, trying to survive in a fully simulated ecosystem of ruins. Every creature has its own behavioural logic – they hunt you, are hunted by apex predators, migrate, sleep, fight. They do not care about you. The game is brutally hard, with a vertical learning curve. But once you cross that threshold, you will find one of the most profound works ever made about the transition from prey to predator. It started with a Metacritic score of 59, but the devs kept updating for seven years. Today it sits at 94% positive on Steam, with the massive expansion *Downpour* and a 2025 DLC, *The Watcher*.

**Öoo**

A caterpillar is swallowed by a giant bird. You must escape before you are digested. Your only tool is a bomb that regenerates infinitely – blast your way through obstacles, trigger mechanisms, and even launch yourself higher with the explosion. About 2–3 hours long, completely wordless. Made by three Japanese indie developers working together. The art style resembles *Animal Well*. It achieved overwhelming positive reviews on Steam in its first week.

**Noita**

Every pixel has its own physical properties – burning, melting, exploding, conducting electricity. You wander through procedurally generated caves collecting wands, then combine spells like programming: split on trigger, delayed cast, projectile modifiers – almost any combination works, including ones that instantly kill yourself. No saves. One life. Back to the start.

**Lobotomy Corporation**

Project Moon's first game. You play the manager of a facility that extracts energy from "Abnormalities" – creatures born from humanity's collective subconscious. Each Abnormality has its own escape mechanics and handling logic. Your employees will die, and they will die horribly. This is where the game's massive urban fantasy world begins.

**Library of Ruina**

Continues from the ending of *Lobotomy Corporation*. Angela builds a library in the ruins, inviting "guests" to visit – those who lose become books. The card‑battle system revolves around "emotion levels": instead of just playing whatever you draw, you accumulate emotion through clashes, and your hand is reshuffled every turn. This is where Project Moon's storytelling truly opens up.

**Limbus Company**

Project Moon's third game, and their first cross‑platform title. You play the manager of a bus, dragging 12 sinners (all familiar faces from the *Lobotomy Corporation* era) through the City to collect golden branches. Turn‑based combat with real‑time clash decisions. Still updating.

**osu!**

An open‑source, community‑driven rhythm game. Four modes (standard, taiko, catch the fruit, piano). Player‑made beatmaps are the core. The entry barrier is low, but the skill ceiling is absurd – playing the same map hundreds of times is normal.

**Casualties: Unknown**

A brutal cave‑exploration survival game. You are a prisoner sent to an alien planet to retrieve lost cargo, exploring 11 layers of caves. Its standout feature is a comprehensive limb and cardiovascular simulation – wounds need bandaging, infections need treatment, blood loss must be managed. Your mood affects your survival odds; excessive pessimism makes everything worse. Three playable characters with different playstyles, 300 discoverable items, and adjustable loot rates in the main menu.  
Still in demo, but already very enjoyable and punishing. Highly recommended for the curious.

**Outer Wilds**

Very good. Very moving. Very healing. A game you can only truly play once.

## Mods

Many games have modding communities. Search **Nexus Mods** – it is the world's largest game mod sharing platform. Almost every popular game has user‑created mods there. It is free; you just need to create an account to download. The free download speed is slow, but it works.
