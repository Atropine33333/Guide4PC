# Appendix 21: Resources

> This appendix shares some useful resources. For anything involving copyright issues, please use your own judgment. The content here is presented as‑is, without endorsement.

## 1. Useful Sites and Software

- **rutor.is**: A Russian site with a wide selection of games, movies, and TV shows. It updates quickly and has a good variety. It is not difficult to use – you can figure it out with a little trial and error.

- **FitGirl Repack**: Specialises in highly compressed game repacks. If your internet connection is slow or your hard drive space is limited, her versions are worth considering – they are very small, and after installation, the games run perfectly. The official domain changes from time to time, so just search for the latest one.

- **Kazumi**: An app for watching anime. It is a powerful media player on its own, but you need to connect third‑party sources (e.g., Yinghua Dongman, DM84). Search for it yourself and follow the official guide. Once configured, watching online is very convenient.

- **AnimeGarden**: If you prefer not to stream or want to download anime music, this is a good place. It is a mirror of the Angel Anime forum – search and access are much faster than on the original site. It mostly provides magnet links, suitable for downloading with a torrent client.

**Baidu Tieba** is also a surprisingly good resource. For obscure, old, or niche games – and fan translation patches – the forums (sub‑communities called "tieba") often have people who actually store and share them. Look for posts where the moderator or a member shares a cloud storage link; dig through the comments, and you will often find something useful. You do not need the standalone app – just use a browser.

**3DM** and **Youmin Xingkong** (GameStar) are long‑standing single‑player game sites with comprehensive resources. However, games downloaded from Youmin often come with pop‑up ads or a custom launcher – annoying, but the games still run. If that bothers you, check the comments to see if someone has posted a link to a "clean" or "portable" version.

## 2. Basic Etiquette

Before downloading anything from a forum or site, it is a good idea to read the comments. People often report things like "the link is dead" or "what is the extraction password?" After you finish downloading, if the forum requires a reply, do not just post "+3" to pad your count – say something useful, like "the link works" or "the download speed is decent." If you act like a leech for too long, eventually there will be no place left to borrow from.

When you encounter someone who shares resources generously on a forum, on Tieba, or in a group – maybe they bought a game with their own money, uploaded it, maybe they spent time creating a fan translation patch, maybe they curated a collection of dozens of old games – do not just download silently. Post a reply. Say "thank you for sharing" or "you are a kind soul." It costs you nothing, but it makes the sharer feel appreciated.

## 3. Potential Problems You May Encounter

When downloading "learning edition" games (unofficial/repack releases), you may run into some odd things:

- **Mojibake (garbled file names)** : A mess of random characters. You may need a special tool to decode them.
- **Split archives**: A pile of `.rar`, `.r01`, `.r02` files. You must download all of them before you can extract the game.
- **Nested archives**: You extract one archive, only to find another archive inside – and maybe another layer after that.
- **Passwords**: An extraction password is required. It is usually hidden in the post (e.g., behind a spoiler tag or in a comment).

These annoyances are actually good learning opportunities. To get the game working, you will actively search for things like "how to decode mojibake file names" or "how to extract lz4 archives". As you search, you will discover that these problems are not that hard. The next time you run into them, you will already know what to do.

## 4. A Delicate Matter

If you are interested in Japanese games or ASMR, **DLsite** is a legitimate platform. There are many tutorials on Bilibili that walk you through creating an account, buying points, waiting for discounts, and using coupons. Supporting the creators is not expensive, and it gives you peace of mind.  
Avoid doing things you should not do. Be discreet and stay out of trouble.

## 5. A Personal Recommendation: A Game I Truly Like

Finally, allow me to indulge in a little personal bias – if you are comfortable with adult games, I highly recommend [Alice in Cradle](https://cn.aliceincradle.net/download/).

![Alice in cradle](/img/AIC.png)

In a nutshell, it is a **side‑scrolling action RPG (ACT + RPG)** , in the Metroidvania style. You play as Noelle, a young elf girl, exploring, fighting, and solving puzzles in a magical fantasy forest. The combat system revolves around **spell casting** – you combine button presses to unleash different spells. The story takes place in a world built on "mana", where elves, orcs, and demons coexist. The character design is thoughtful, the pixel art is exquisite, and the music is excellent.

A word of warning: the game is **R‑18 rated**, with some adult content (e.g., defeat CG scenes). There is a "safe mode" in the settings that you can turn on. If this bothers you, look elsewhere. If it does not, you will find a doujin (indie) game that is genuinely well‑crafted in terms of gameplay.

You can search for "Alice In Cradle" on Moegirl (Mengniang Baike) – the entry there is very detailed. On Bilibili, there is an official account and many guide videos. The game is still being updated, and a free demo is available for download.

If you try it and end up liking it, then we share the same taste.

**One final thought**

There is no shortage of resources online. What matters is how you find them, how you use them, and how you treat the people who share them. Stay curious, stay polite, and that will serve you better than hoarding hundreds of gigabytes of files.
