## A Closing Poem

I remember the first time I opened this machine. The keys were a field of unknown furrows. The screen, a blank ocean. My finger hovered — afraid that one wrong touch would send me wandering forever.

Then someone told me a simple thing: *Nothing inside is a tiger. No protocol is a net you cannot untie. No script is a spell — only a language waiting to be read. Press on. You'll find your way.*

So I did.

I learned to carve my own folders from the wild disk, to name each branch so I could find it again. When the screen spat out gibberish, I learned it was just a missing map. When an error flashed red, I learned to ask the logs — and they answered, quietly, like old walls giving up a hidden door.

I walked into the network. Seven layers of invisible silk. NAT, firewall, proxy — each a gate, not a wall. I learned to set my own path across the ocean, to let my packets choose their own currents. Clash in the background, a quiet compass.

I learned how light becomes numbers. H.264, AV1, containers, codecs. OBS to catch the passing sky. FFmpeg in my palm — a knife that cuts any shape.

And then I found the great invisible city — open source. Millions of hands passing fire. No one asks your name. They only ask: *can you carry this forward?* I learned the licenses, the forks that return to the same river. I found the mirrors where every stream gathers, so a student anywhere can drink.

I learned Markdown — to write without heavy armour. Git — to track every heartbeat of my work, from one machine to every corner of the earth. SSH — a whisper that crosses mountains and opens a window on a distant desktop. And tunnelling — to slip through the back ways and sit down at the great table inside.

Now I understand.

When I first entered this box, I was standing in a dark room. Now I carry my own candle. What I feared, I use. What confused me has become my daily bread.

I am not wiser than others. I only put one foot down, then another. And I am not alone — there is something else now, a quiet companion I can ask: *AI, show me the way.*

*Alas!*

So here is my short creed:

I will build a house of gold for my data.  
I will walk through every cloud without a map.  
I will bend light and shadow until they please my eye.  
I will carve time itself into small, saved moments.

This is not paradise. It is work.  
It is not a shortcut. It is long, and sometimes hard.

But I have already begun.  
I will not look back —  
except to see you.

Beyond this field, I see a new sun.  
And when you step onto the same path,  
I will already be there,  
hand outstretched.

— *A fellow traveller, who once stood where you stand, and now walks ahead — but never so far that I cannot turn back, take your hand, and say: you can do this too.*
